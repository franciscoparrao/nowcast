\opening{Dear Editors,}

On behalf of all authors, I am pleased to submit the manuscript entitled
\textbf{``nowcast: a forcing-agnostic Rust engine for dynamic geohazard
nowcasting, and what rainfall resolution buys an intensity--duration trigger''} for consideration as a
research article in \textit{Environmental Modelling \& Software}.

Susceptibility maps for landslides and floods are static, and empirical rainfall
thresholds say \emph{when} but not \emph{where}. Operational early warning needs
both, yet existing systems weld one forcing product, one trigger and one
susceptibility model into a single chain. The manuscript presents
\textbf{nowcast}, an open, dependency-light Rust engine that writes time-varying
hazard as a susceptibility field times a composable trigger behind a single
interchangeable forcing interface, and then uses that interface as an
experimental instrument: holding the model fixed, it varies only the resolution
of the forcing and measures how much of the attainable skill that resolution
decides.

We believe the manuscript fits the journal for the following reasons:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A generalizable software contribution.} The forcing interface
    decouples the hazard logic from the data source and the trigger family.
    Rainfall, routed discharge, snowmelt and InSAR deformation enter through one
    trait; triggers compose; physical refinement (2-D shallow-water inundation,
    agent-based debris-flow runout) runs on demand only where the cheap nowcast
    alerts; and every alert carries an exact, closed-form attribution to terrain
    and trigger. Section~2 positions this design against OpenMI, BMI and OMS3 and
    against operational chains such as LHASA and CTRL-T.

    \item \textbf{A methods result with a negative finding reported in full.}
    On the R\'io Maipo basin (157 dated landslides, 38 years of daily gridded
    rainfall), distributing the daily forcing and weighting it by a real
    machine-learning susceptibility raster does not lift discrimination above
    chance (ROC-AUC $\approx 0.48$), and the full ensemble of published
    intensity--duration thresholds does not rescue it. Diagnostics show the
    null accuses the inventory as much as the forcing, so the resolution claim
    is not rested on it. A controlled experiment whose labels come from an
    independent failure mechanism isolates the cause: aggregating the identical
    field to daily resolution collapses the catch rate on burst-driven failures
    from 0.46 to 0.00 while leaving accumulation-driven failures untouched.

    \item \textbf{A timing result at high resolution, stated as such.} With
    half-hourly GPM IMERG forcing the same trigger places the threshold crossing
    at a timestamp hours ahead of three documented debris flows in opposite
    Chilean climates; without an event onset hour and an off-event crossing rate
    these are timing demonstrations, not skill scores. A
    single-factor decomposition separates the rainfall product from its
    resolution, and an intercept-sensitivity analysis shows the contrast does not
    depend on the threshold chosen. A discharge path checked against observed
    streamflow discriminates floods out of sample (ROC-AUC 0.90), but no better
    than a 3-day rainfall sum or persistence.

    \item \textbf{Reproducibility.} The engine, its backtesting framework and
    all its examples are open source (MIT/Apache-2.0) at
    \url{https://github.com/franciscoparrao/nowcast}, archived as version 0.2.0
    at \url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23198956}. The 24 examples of the
    core crate build and run offline and, with the data-extraction scripts
    included in the repository, regenerate Sections~5.1--5.7. The Software Availability section states precisely which
    results reproduce from the archived snapshot alone.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Related work by the first author.} The engine builds on a family of sibling
Rust models that it couples through the forcing interface. Two of them concern
this journal and we want to declare them explicitly to avoid any appearance of
overlap. (i)~\emph{SurtGIS}, the geospatial raster substrate used for GeoTIFF
input and output, was published in \textit{Environmental Modelling \& Software}
(Parra, 2026, vol.~204, 107102, \url{https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2026.107102})
and is cited as such. (ii)~\emph{hydroflux}, the 2-D shallow-water solver used
for on-demand inundation refinement, is described in a separate manuscript
currently under review in this journal; the present manuscript mentions it only
as a coupled component, does not reproduce its verification, and cites no
unpublished result from it. We will gladly provide the hydroflux manuscript to the
handling editor or reviewers on request. Neither sibling paper contains the
engine, the backtesting framework or the resolution experiments reported here.

This manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration
by any other journal. The authors declare no competing interests. The use of a generative
AI tool in drafting and editing is declared in the manuscript, as the journal
requires.
